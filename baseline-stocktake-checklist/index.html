The baseline stocktake · step one of every budget

know where you stand before you budget

Eight small steps and one calculator. You'll finish with your monthly position: a surplus, a shortfall, or somewhere in between, plus how long your savings would last. It takes about 20 minutes with your bank statements to hand.

Your numbers never leave your phone. They're saved on this device only, and you can clear them at the bottom.

  1. Step 1

    choose how far back to look

    Twelve months is best. It catches once-a-year bills, Christmas and the quiet months. Three months is a solid start, and one month is still worth doing. If you only have a month or two, the calculator has a spot for your yearly bills.

    Use complete months only. Start with the last full month and count back.

  2. Step 2

    gather your transactions

    Pull the transactions for your window from every account: everyday, credit cards, buy now pay later, loans and savings. Most banks let you export a CSV or spreadsheet. If yours doesn't, the monthly summary in your app works too. Don't forget cash.

    Don't judge anything yet. This step is only collecting.

  3. Step 3

    add up what came in

    Total all the money that landed in your accounts over the window, after tax: wages, side income, government payments, rent you receive. Leave out transfers between your own accounts. Take refunds off the spending they relate to instead of counting them as income.

  4. Step 4

    add up what you spent on living

    Total everything you spent: rent, bills, groceries, transport, eating out, subscriptions, kids, pets, the lot. Leave out two things so nothing is counted twice: money you moved into savings, and payments toward debt. Those are next.

    Then work out how much of it was fixed: the bills that are the same every month, like rent, power, insurance, childcare and phone. The calculator uses it to show what's left to live on once the fixed stuff is paid.

  5. Step 5

    total your savings and debt payments

    Two more totals for the window. First, money you moved into savings or investments. Second, money you paid toward debt: credit cards, loans, buy now pay later, student loans and mortgage payments. Rent counts as spending in step 4, but mortgage payments belong here.

  6. Step 6

    find your monthly averages

    Divide each total by the number of months in your window. Three months of data: total ÷ 3. Twelve months: total ÷ 12.

    Example: $9,600 of income over 3 months is $9,600 ÷ 3 = $3,200 a month. $26,400 of spending over 12 months is $26,400 ÷ 12 = $2,200 a month.

    The calculator below does the dividing for you, so you can enter your totals as they are.

    If one month had a big one-off, like a car repair or a holiday, you can leave it out or keep it in. Keep it in for a more cautious picture. Pick one and stay consistent.

  7. Step 7

    check today's balances

    Two snapshots as of today, with no dividing. Total savings held: cash and savings you could reach if you needed to, leaving out money locked away for retirement. Total debt owed: every balance outstanding, including your mortgage if you have one.

  8. Step 8

    plug in your numbers

    Add everything to the calculator below and read your monthly position. Then pick a Sunday next month to do it all again. One stocktake is a snapshot. Doing it monthly is how you see what's actually changing.
